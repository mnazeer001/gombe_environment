Programmes & Projects

Flagship initiatives restoring land, controlling erosion, greening our cities and building climate resilience.

Portfolio

Our active programmes

Filter by theme to explore what the Ministry and its partners are delivering across the eleven local government areas.

Flagship
Afforestation 2019 – date

Gombe Goes Green (3G) Initiative

Statewide tree planting, urban greening and shelterbelt establishment, with school green clubs and nursery enterprises for youth and women. Over five million trees planted to date.

Annual target progress: 78%
World Bank
Land restoration 2022 – date

ACReSAL — Agro-Climatic Resilience in Semi-Arid Landscapes

Catchment management plans, reclamation and afforestation of degraded land, borrow pits and landfills, roadside plantations, and community revolving funds for climate-smart livelihoods.

Implementation progress: 65%
Erosion
Watershed Legacy & follow-on

Gully Erosion Control — GSU, FCE & Doma sites

Engineering and bio-remediation works across three major gully complexes stretching nearly 50km, protecting schools, homes, farmland and roads worth billions of naira.

Doma site works: 70%
Circular economy
Waste-to-wealth 2024 – date

Materials Recovery & Recycling Facility

Conversion of a legacy dumpsite into a sorting, baling and recycling hub, supporting informal waste pickers with equipment, safety gear and off-take linkages.

Phase 2 expansion: 55%
Nurseries
Seedling production Annual

State Nursery Expansion Programme

Upgrading zonal nurseries with boreholes, shade netting and improved germplasm to raise production of economic species such as moringa, mango, cashew, neem and gum arabic.

Season production target: 82%
Great Green Wall
Anti-desertification Ongoing

Great Green Wall Shelterbelts & Conservation

Shelterbelt establishment and biodiversity protection in frontline communities, with grazing reserves, fuel-efficient stoves and alternative livelihood support.

Belt establishment: 48%

Get involved

Adopt a site, plant a legacy

Corporate organisations, alumni associations, faith groups and development partners can adopt a shelterbelt, school compound, park or erosion site. The Ministry provides seedlings, technical supervision and monitoring; partners fund establishment and maintenance.

  • Named recognition at the adopted site
  • Independent survival-rate monitoring and reporting
  • Community employment during establishment and aftercare
